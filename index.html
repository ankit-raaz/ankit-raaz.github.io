About Me

I am a PhD student in Quantitative Biosciences at Georgia Tech, working in the Spatial Ecology & Paleontology Lab under the supervision of Dr. Jenny McGuire. My research integrates paleontology, fire ecology, and spatial analysis to understand how climate change and megafires reshape mammalian functional trait diversity and community structure over long temporal scales.

My work combines paleofire data (charcoal records) with mammal fossil data to investigate trait-environment relationships, functional distance metrics, and ecological legacies from the Pleistocene to present. I use advanced computational approaches including R, Julia, and circuit theory modeling to analyze large spatial datasets.

Research Projects

Disruption in Trait-Environment Relationships in African Large Mammals

With Dr. Jenny McGuire (Georgia Tech) & Dr. Michelle Lawing (Texas A&M)

Quantifying trait-environment relationships using ecometric modeling and evaluating point connectivity using Omniscape connectivity modeling. Developed novel methods for analyzing ecometric anomaly significance.

Paleofire & Mammalian Functional Diversity

Collaboration with University of Amsterdam & Aarhus University

Integrating paleofire database (charcoal records) with mammal fossil data from Neotoma to understand how megafires and climate change affect functional trait diversity across temporal and spatial scales.

Patterns of Ecospace Utilization in Marine Invertebrates (MS Thesis)

With Dr. Subhronil Mondal (IISER Kolkata) & Prof. Phillip Novack-Gottshall (Benedictine University)

Quantified ecological diversity using theoretical ecospace framework. Analyzed ecospace structuring in the aftermath of the Permian-Triassic mass extinction and during the Mesozoic Marine Revolution. Used machine learning to classify models of ecological diversification.
